The main challenge then becomes designing a good proposal distribution $q(x'|x)$: we want proposals that can efficiently move the chain toward high-probability regions.

This gives us our first alternative perspective to RL:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Instead of learning a better distribution, can we sample better from the distribution we already have?}
}
$$

This perspective naturally leads to other sampling-based approaches, such as importance sampling and Sequential Monte Carlo, which we discuss next.

The efficiency of this method lies in the quality of the proposal distribution $q(x'\lim x)$: if we always sample a state $x'$ with low $p(x)$, then we will rarely move to the new state.

To increase the acceptance rate of the move, a natural idea is to take the gradient direction: $x' = x + \eta \nabla\log p(x) + \zeta$, where $\eta$ is the step size and $\zeta$ is random noise. This sampling method is known as [Langevin dynamics](https://friedmanroy.github.io/blog/2022/Langevin/).
